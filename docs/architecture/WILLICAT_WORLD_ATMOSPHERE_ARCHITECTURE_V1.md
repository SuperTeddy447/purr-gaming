# WilliCat world atmosphere architecture V1 — laboratory contract

Status: **development proof, not a Home V3 migration or final-art approval**. The locked Staggered Salon scene remains authoritative. The [lab scene](../../scenes/dev/world_atmosphere_lighting_lab_v2.tscn) inherits it and adds only atmosphere-owned nodes. Runtime images are in [the review pack](../../artifacts/prototype_review/world_atmosphere_lighting_lab_v2/README.md).

## Composition and ownership

`AtmosphereState` stores five semantic IDs: location, season, time, weather and optional event. The native [catalog](../../scenes/dev/atmosphere/atmosphere_catalog.tres) contains typed Godot Resources. `AtmosphereEvaluator.evaluate(catalog, state)` returns transient render values; it has no room coordinates or actor rules. Invalid IDs or disallowed location/season pairs are rejected without mutating the current state. No country-specific branch appears in gameplay or director code. These country labels are narrow reference scenarios, **not** models of whole countries or cultural palettes.

`WorldAtmosphereDirector` binds to a room's authored CanvasModulate, DirectionalLight2D, Lamp and Window nodes through exported paths. It exposes `set_location`, `set_season`, `set_time_of_day`, `set_weather`, `set_event_lighting`, `clear_event_lighting` and `transition_to_atmosphere(state, seconds)`. The last operation interpolates evaluated values, then commits the new IDs and emits `transition_finished`; a new request cancels the old tween. `_exit_tree` kills any in-flight tween. A room can provide other render adapters later without actor changes. The lab intentionally contains only one lamp/window adapter, not a global singleton or climate engine.

Room visual ownership: illustrated neutral architecture remains in `Architecture`; fixture-owned PointLight2D follows `AtmosphereLamp`; `WindowLightingController` overlays the authored opening and drives a second unshadowed PointLight2D; contact shadow follows its chair/worker parent; the existing HUD CanvasLayer remains outside CanvasModulate. The fixed lab window overlay at (585, 446) is **scene-authored** for the locked layout, not director logic. Moving a lamp/chair moves its light/shadow because those effects are children. A future room binds its own fixtures and exterior window art. WindowPerch gameplay position is unchanged.

## Shadow and light decisions

Static architecture art may keep intrinsic form shading and soft, non-directional AO, but not long sun shadows. Movable furniture and characters get small owner-local contact shadows. The lab has zero shadow-enabled lights and zero LightOccluder2D nodes: Godot DirectionalLight2D's infinite cast shadows are not accepted as the room default. A later selective lamp-shadow test may add fixture-local occluders only if a real visual need outweighs mobile cost. Sun rotation is a direction cue; because flat sprites otherwise wash out, this lab applies only 18% of evaluated direct energy. This was reduced after real viewport captures showed blown-out morning art. The actor is still readable at night, though this placeholder proof cannot certify final Mochi contrast.

Day/night and clear/rain are runtime switches. Weather changes direct/diffuse light, window rain/exterior visibility, wetness/FX hooks and lamp demand; the current rain visual is a small deterministic pane overlay, not finished weather FX or wet-surface material. The evaluator emits `weather_fx`, `atmosphere_fx` and `event_fx` identifiers for future scene-local adapters. Event tint/lamp bonus is additive and clearing it exactly restores the underlying four-axis output. Exterior changes are a neutral color/pane treatment with a crude foliage-density hook; small authored outside variants should be assigned through location/season/window data later, never a complete Home replacement. The review pack includes a **same season/time/weather** Japan-reference vs Thailand-reference pair so location differences are not merely differences in the other axes.

## Material trial

[Material response probe](../../scenes/dev/atmosphere/material_response_probe.tscn) compares the **same existing V2 jade chair texture** under (A) painted sprite + unshadowed light, (B) a deliberately mild procedural normal map + light, and (C) a broad stylized shader + light. [Real Godot capture](../../artifacts/prototype_review/world_atmosphere_lighting_lab_v2/14_material_response_probe.png) shows B darkens/changes the painted form more than is obviously useful; C retains more of the original look, but this is not a final verdict. The simple normal map is a technical stand-in, not an authored production map. Neither approach is mandated for cats or furniture. Neutral painted sprites plus restrained local lights remain the safest baseline; use optional material-specific tests for brass, ceramic and glass.

Material intent: wood keeps painted grain with modest warm response; cream plaster stays diffuse; jade paint keeps color identity under both warm and cool ambience; aged brass/ceramic/glass may use carefully bounded highlights; textiles and plants primarily retain authored matte shape, with foliage variants for season. No photoreal PBR requirement.

## Persistence and extension

Persist location/season/time/weather IDs, active event ID if the event itself persists, and per-lamp manual override. Do **not** persist derived colors, energies, window brightness, particles or light positions. On load, resolve IDs against versioned catalog data and choose a documented fallback if an ID was removed. Actors query semantic world objects only. Future rooms can reuse the catalog/evaluator/director and bind their own lamps/windows; exterior art family and foliage IDs are data hooks. Seasonal assets may swap outside foliage, a plant variant, small decor/textiles or pastries, never navigation/object slots/entire room geometry as a side effect of lighting.

## Mobile tiers and open gates

BASE: CanvasModulate, subtle unshadowed sun, 0–1 local practical lights, contact blobs, static exterior variant. STANDARD: one window light plus a few visible lamps and bounded pane rain/particles. ENHANCED: selective per-material response and a small number of **measured** local shadow casters. No full-screen post-process, universal normal maps or shadowed directional sun in the baseline. Profile interpolation changes properties and is negligible relative to fill-rate, but that is an expectation, **not a measurement**.

Desktop captures prove architecture and a first visual contrast gate, not shipping performance. One live **desktop game-process** monitor read during the lab returned 119 FPS, 102 draw calls and 406 rendered objects (Godot 4.7.2 mobile renderer on Apple M2 Pro); this is a single observation, not a p50/p95 benchmark, and the editor's own monitor is not a game measurement. A low/mid-tier physical phone still needs p50/p95 frame times, GPU/CPU, thermals, memory, overdraw, weather particle count, light count, transition hitches and night readability at 9:16 and tall portrait. Final art and at least one real-device test remain open. The referenced `WILLICAT_HOME_V3_VISUAL_TARGET_V1` file was not present in this checkout during the audit, so its specific painted lighting cannot be certified here.
